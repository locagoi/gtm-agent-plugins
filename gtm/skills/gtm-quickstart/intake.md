# The intake interview

Everything the build consumes, and nothing it does not. Work through it with the human
before creating a single asset. **Do not fill a gap with a guess** — an invented positioning
produces copy that is specific, confident and wrong, at volume.

Where an answer is missing, say what it costs: "without a case study number, the proof asset
will be weak and step two of the sequence has nothing to carry."

---

## A — Offer

| Question | Why the build needs it |
|---|---|
| Your core offer in **one sentence** | becomes the `offer` asset; goes in every sequence step |
| What does it produce, in a number and a timeframe? | separates a promise from a claim |
| What is the lowest-commitment thing you can give upfront? | the CTA that validates instead of selling |
| Pricing model, in one line (incl. anything that lowers risk) | the entry-barrier line in step one |

Format that works: **`[benefit] for [ICP]`**. If it needs a paragraph, it is not defined yet.

## B — ICP

Keep it to **1–3 ICPs**. More than three is not a strategy, it is an absence of one.

| Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| Industry / segment | ≤ 6, in **one fixed spelling** you will reuse everywhere |
| Region / countries | **≤ 5**. Broader lists reply about half as often |
| Employee range | a real window, not "any" |
| Revenue range | where known |
| Enterprise / mid-market / SMB | drives persona and channel |
| **Good-fit criteria** | the yes/no checks a model can apply |
| **Bad-fit criteria** | the disqualifiers — as important as the good-fit list, and usually missing |
| Lookalike companies | 3–5 names of your best current customers |

The test: can a model decide fit from these criteria alone, without asking you? If not, they
are not criteria yet.

## C — Buying signals

**3–5 per ICP.** This is the difference between a plausible approach and an interruption.

| Question | Examples |
|---|---|
| What tells you a company has this problem **right now**? | hiring for the function, funding, new leadership, market expansion, a tech change, website visit, engagement with your content |
| Where is that visible? | job board, news, LinkedIn, your own site |
| How fresh must it be? | default: hot 3–7 days, cold after ~28 |

If there is no observable signal for this ICP, say so. The campaign then runs cold, and you
should plan a lower reply rate rather than pretend otherwise.

## D — Proof

| Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| Which customer had the most striking result? | name, **number with a unit**, timeframe. "20 auf 4 Minuten pro Lead, seit Januar" — not "deutlich schneller" |
| What was the value BEFORE? | a benefit without a baseline cannot be checked, and an unverifiable number is worse than none |
| How big is that customer, and in which industry? | a reference only proves something to a comparable company |
| 1–3 case studies per ICP | matched to company size and industry so the right one can be selected per row |
| What did they say about it? | a quote in their words beats your summary |

Benefit format that survives contact with a reader: **`[verb] [metric] by [amount]`** —
"reduce research time by 60–70 %". Not "significantly improves efficiency".

## E — Personas

**2–4 per ICP**: one decision maker, one or two influencers, at most one end user.

| Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| Job title / role | the exact titles, plus the ones to exclude ("Assistant", "Intern") |
| Decision maker or influencer? | changes the ask, not just the wording |
| What are they measured on? | the lever your offer actually moves |
| **Challenges, 4–6** | **verbatim, in their words** — not your paraphrase. This is the raw material for every hook, and a paraphrase reads like a brochure. Format: `[process] [negative verb] [consequence]` |
| **Benefits, 4–6** | what changes for them personally |
| Typical objections | the 3 you hear most, and what defuses each |
| CTA that fits them | a question that validates rather than sells |

Common patterns: SaaS → Head of Sales + RevOps · Enterprise → VP Sales + CRO + Sales Ops ·
SMB → Founder + Head of Sales.

## F — Channel and identities

| Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| Which channel do these people actually answer? | pick **one** to start. The second channel comes after the first has a measured reply rate |
| Sending identity (name, role, address/profile) | a real person; the reply lands with them |
| Email: which domains, warmed how long? | never the main domain; 3 weeks warmup minimum |
| LinkedIn: which account, connected? | a real account with real ban risk |
| WhatsApp: dedicated number? | never the private or main business number |
| Booking link | goes in the *reply*, not in a cold body |
| Language and form of address | DACH B2B: formal "Sie" unless told otherwise |

## G — Boundaries

| Question | Why |
|---|---|
| **Blacklist**: domains, companies, individuals | existing customers, active deals, competitors, anyone who asked to be left alone |
| Existing contacts to skip | avoids the "we're already talking" reply |
| Compliance constraints | industry rules, works-council agreements, regional restrictions |
| Who approves what goes out? | the human in the loop, named |

The blacklist is not a nice-to-have. Writing cold to an account your colleague is closing is
the single most expensive mistake in the list.

## H — What exists already

| Question | Why |
|---|---|
| Current outreach: what works, what does not? | the fastest source of a real angle |
| Best-performing copy you already have | do not rewrite what already works |
| CRM data on won deals | the most reliable ICP evidence there is |
| Average deal size and sales cycle | tells you what volume is actually worth |

---

## Completeness bar

Before stage 2, you should have:

- [ ] 1 offer, in one sentence, with a number
- [ ] 1–3 ICPs, each with good-fit **and** bad-fit criteria
- [ ] 3–5 buying signals per ICP
- [ ] 1–3 case studies per ICP, with numbers
- [ ] 2–4 personas per ICP, with challenges and benefits in their words
- [ ] 1 channel chosen, with a verified sender behind it
- [ ] a blacklist
- [ ] a named human who approves sends

Anything unticked is a known weakness to state out loud — not a reason to invent the answer.
